It is deliberately scoped larger than the time available.We are evaluating three things: what you chose to build, what you chose not to build, and whether the thing you built holds up when someone pokes it. It moves ambient, chilled and frozen products from eight distribution centres to roughly seven hundred retail outlets across five regions in India. Channels are general trade, modern trade, HORECA and e-commerce dark stores. Annual revenue is in the region of INR 900 crore.Kestrel runs on a patchwork. Orders arrive from a field sales mobile app, an ERP web portal, and a partner API used by the e-commerce accounts. Deliveries are tracked by two different telematics vendors. Freight is billed by five carriers through a partner logistics platform. Competitor pricing is watched by an analyst who checks a public price tracker manually, once a week, and pastes screenshots into a WhatsApp group.The Head of Supply Chain Operations has asked for a control tower. Below is her brief, verbatim, followed by a note from the commercial side.\---## 2\. What we are asking you to buildYou decide.Read the brief above. Work out what a system that answers it looks like, work out how much of that you can build properly in the time you have, and build that.We are deliberately not giving you a feature list. Reading an ambiguous brief and deciding what to build is most of this job. Handing you the answer would remove the only part of the exercise we cannot assess any other way.Two things are not optional.**A working system.** Something a person can open and use, starting from a clean checkout on a documented command. Not a notebook, not a slide deck, not a design.**Two documents in the repository.*** `README.md`. How to run it. Cold start, one machine, no tribal knowledge.* `DECISIONS.md`, one page maximum. What you built. What you deliberately did not build. What you assumed where the brief was unclear or contradicted itself. What you would do next with two more weeks. What breaks first in production.We read `DECISIONS.md` before we read the code.Where the brief is ambiguous, make a judgement and record it. We are more interested in the judgement than in the answer you land on.## 5\. The kind of thing Divya asksIllustrative, not a specification. Some of these may not be worth building for. Use them to sense check whatever you do build; we will run a different set against your submission.1. SubmissionA single GitHub repository, public or shared with the address HR gave you. Nothing else. Expect to be asked why you made specific choices, what you would do differently, and what happens to your design when the data volume goes up by two orders of magnitude.A modest submission that is well understood and honestly described will go further than an ambitious one you cannot account for.\---## 8\. Notes on the dataThe database is synthetic and generated for this exercise. It is not clean. Neither is anything you will meet in the field. Every distributor we have worked with has had the same categories of problem in their data, and finding them is part of the job rather than an obstacle to it.You are not expected to fix the data. You are expected to notice.
